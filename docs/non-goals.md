# Discipler — Non-Goals

This document defines what Discipler is deliberately not trying to become.

Absence from this list does not authorize a new feature. New product capabilities still require an approved requirement.

## Discipler Is Not the Relationship

Discipler does not replace mentorship, discipleship, pastoral care, prayer, or human conversation.

The software exists to help relationships begin, continue, and receive support when needed.

## Discipler Does Not Automatically Choose People for the Pastor

The system may calculate suggested pairings from approved data such as availability.

Suggestions do not become relationships until the pastor creates the pairing.

The suggestion engine should not quietly evolve into an opaque compatibility or personality-scoring system.

## Discipler Does Not Need a Complex Pairing Algorithm

V1 pairing suggestions are based on simple availability overlap.

Do not add personality matching, AI compatibility scores, demographic matching, inferred spiritual compatibility, or other weighting unless explicitly approved later.

## Discipler Does Not Enroll People Without Consent

Importing, synchronizing, or discovering a person in another system does not equal enrollment or communication consent.

## Discipler Is Not a General Church Management System

Discipler should not reproduce unrelated functionality from platforms such as Planning Center.

It is focused on operating discipleship relationships.

## Discipler Is Not Dependent on Planning Center

Planning Center may reduce duplicate administrative work.

The core ministry must remain operable without it.

## Discipler Does Not Needlessly Expand Third-Party Access

An integration should receive only the access required for an approved workflow.

The product should not collect unrelated church information merely because an API exposes it.

## Discipler Is Not a Surveillance System

The goal is not to monitor every interaction between participants.

Discipler should collect the information needed to understand whether relationships are operating, how they are going, and where care may be needed.

## Discipler Is Not a Punishment System

A missed meeting, unanswered message, concern, pause, or exit should not automatically be framed as wrongdoing.

Care signals exist to help pastors support people, not score or shame them.

## Demographics Do Not Automatically Drive Individual Decisions

Age, gender, or other approved demographic information may support aggregate ministry analysis.

Discipler should not automatically determine pairing suitability, participant value, pastoral priority, or discipleship eligibility from demographic characteristics.

## Discipler Does Not Automatically Aggregate Qualitative Concerns

Individual concerns should be available to the pastor as they arise.

Cross-ministry concern summarization happens only when explicitly requested.

## Discipler Does Not Need Heavy Participant Administration

Mentors, mentees, group leaders, and group participants should not have to maintain complex records or repeatedly log into software simply to participate in discipleship.

## A Kickoff Event Is Not an Event-Management Product

Discipler may support a mentorship kickoff with communication templates, resources, and simple event details.

Do not add ticketing, venue management, generalized calendars, complex RSVP systems, or unrelated event tooling without an approved requirement.

## No Speculative SaaS Features

Do not add features because they are common in SaaS products or because an AI agent thinks they might be useful.

Examples requiring explicit approval include:

- gamification
- badges
- streaks
- points
- social feeds
- participant leaderboards
- generalized CRM functionality
- speculative AI recommendations
- opaque engagement scores
- unnecessary dashboards
- unnecessary communication channels
- complex role systems beyond the ministry need
- automated demographic-based pairing
- generalized event management

## No Implementation by Inference

If a material product behavior is ambiguous, surface the ambiguity.

Do not invent a requirement merely to keep implementation moving.

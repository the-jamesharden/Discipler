# Discipler — The Mentor Experience

*From invitation to weekly discipleship, end to end.*

## 1. Getting In — Two Pathways

- **Invited:** The mentor receives an email from their pastor: *"You've been invited to join [Church]'s discipleship ministry as a mentor,"* with a link to the church's private form.
- **Inbound:** The mentor scans the church's QR code (or types the short link) from a bulletin, slide, or poster.

Both pathways lead to the same form, tokenized to their specific church — so submitting it places them directly in that church's ministry.

## 2. The Form

The mentor fills out their name, phone number, and weekly availability. Availability is a simple grid — days of the week across the top, time blocks down the side — where they tap every window that could work for them. Multiple selections are encouraged, and the more they select, the better their pairing options. Two consent lines appear at the bottom of the form:

> *"By submitting, you consent to receive text messages from the ministry."*
>
> *"Your phone number is shared with the ministry and with your paired mentee."*

## 3. Confirmation Text

Immediately on submitting, the mentor receives a welcome text from Discipler confirming their opt-in and setting expectations: they're in the pool, their pastor will pair them soon, message frequency, and STOP/HELP instructions. Replying confirms their number is real and live.

## 4. Pairing

When the pastor pairs them with a mentee, the mentor gets a text:

> *"You have been paired with [Mentee] from [Church]. Do you agree to this pairing? Reply 1 for yes, 2 for no."*

- Both parties reply yes → the pair goes active, and the mentor receives the mentee's **phone number** so they can connect directly.
- Either party declines → both quietly return to the pool. Declines are private; only the pastor sees that a pairing didn't go through.

## 5. The Mentor Dashboard

The mentor logs in to the website with their phone number — they receive a one-time code by text, no password to remember. Their view shows:

- **Ministry resources:** the church's discipleship materials, uploaded by the pastor
- **Mentee availability:** the time slots the mentee marked on their form, making it easy to schedule the weekly meetup

## 6. The Weekly Rhythm

The mentor and mentee meet weekly at their chosen time. At the end of each week, the mentor receives a check-in text:

> *"Did you meet with [Mentee] this week? Reply 1 for yes, 2 for no."*

If they met:

> *"How did the meeting go? Reply A for good, B for okay, C for concern."*

If they reply **C**:

> *"How can we be praying for you? What was the specific concern?"*

Every check-in closes with:

> *"Thank you. We appreciate your response."*

**If they didn't meet (reply 2):** that's not a failure report — it's the signal the whole system exists to catch. A single missed week is normal life; the check-in simply closes with the thank-you text. Repeated missed weeks quietly flag the pair on the pastor's dashboard so the pastor can step in and help, long before the relationship fades out on its own.

**If they don't reply at all:** a gentle reminder text follows the next day. Continued silence feeds the same stall flag — no reply is treated as a signal, never as an escape hatch, and never as something the mentor gets scolded for.

**If a couple of weeks go by without meeting,** the next check-in offers a way to talk about it: *"Reply 1 to keep going, 2 to talk with your pastor about this pairing."* The off-ramp appears only when it's actually relevant, and choosing it simply lets the pastor know a conversation would help.

Concerns flow straight to the pastor's dashboard for follow-up — the mentor never has to chase anyone down to raise a flag. Their whole job is the relationship; Discipler handles the rest.

**Once a month,** the first check-in of the calendar month carries a short reminder — *"Reply STOP to opt out. Msg&data rates may apply."* Every other message stays clean.

## 7. The Six-Week Rhythm Check

Every six weeks, the mentor gets a question that gives the relationship a natural checkpoint:

> *"You've been meeting with [Mentee] for 6 weeks. Is this still a good season for it? Reply 1 to keep going, 2 to talk with your pastor."*

No one has to invent an awkward conversation about whether a pairing is still working — Discipler asks on a schedule, and either answer is a good answer.

## 8. Stepping Back or Stepping Out

Seasons change, and there are always graceful options that don't require an awkward conversation:

- **Reply PAUSE** to any Discipler text. A confirmation comes back — *"Pause your check-ins for 4 weeks? Reply YES to confirm"* — so nothing happens by accident. Confirmed, the check-ins stop, the pairing stays intact, and everything resumes automatically when the pause ends. Reply START anytime to come back early.
- **Use the dashboard.** Every check-in carries a link to a page where the mentor can pause, step out of the pairing, or leave the program entirely. It requires logging in with a one-time code, so it can never be triggered by a stray tap.
- **Reply HELP** anytime to get the options back, including how to pause and how to reach a pastor.
- **Ending well:** when a mentorship reaches its natural end, either the mentor or the pastor can close the pair. Texts stop, the history is preserved, and the mentor returns to the pool ready for the next mentee if they want to be. Participant-initiated exits show on the pastor's dashboard as such — informative, not accusatory.
- **Updating info:** availability and phone numbers change — the mentor updates both from their dashboard anytime, and the changes flow through to scheduling and texts automatically.

**If STOP gets used by accident:** replying STOP ends all Discipler texts immediately — carriers enforce that, so nothing more can be sent. But the pairing is not deleted. Texting **START** (that exact word) restores everything, including the existing pairing, with no need to re-confirm. The ministry's help page explains this, and the pastor also sees the opt-out and can reach out personally.
